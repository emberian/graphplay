# Applied Graphplay: Microsoft's Majorana-1 Topological Qubit Chip

*A stretch-case disassembly of the Feb 2024 Majorana-1 architecture through
the Graphplay tower (Towers 3, 6, 7).*

## 1. Graphplay in one paragraph

Graphplay is a Lean 4 + Mathlib formalisation of a hierarchy ("the tower")
of generalisations of the equitable-partition / quotient-graph story for
continuous-time quantum walks (CTQW).  Tower 0 is plain `SimpleGraph`s,
Tower 1 weighted-Hermitian adjacency, Tower 2 chiral signings (U(1) lattice
gauge fields on edges), **Tower 3** the operator-algebraic / non-commutative
coherent-algebra upgrade (`QuantumGraph n`), Tower 4 measurable graphons,
Tower 5 the categorical scaffolding with the headline
`Quotient.preservesFilteredColimits`, **Tower 6** a sheaf of unital
\*-algebras over a topological parameter space (`SheafGraph X`), and **Tower
7** the bicategorical / ∞-categorical truncation where equitable partitions
become coherent idempotents and PST becomes a 2-cell in the unitary
∞-groupoid.  The motif everywhere: a symmetry/partition of a "host" object
induces a *quotient*; spectral / dynamical / topological properties on the
quotient lift back to "cell-uniform" properties on the host.  See
`Graphplay/Tower6.lean`, `Graphplay/Tower7.lean`,
`Graphplay/Dowsing/NonCommutativeCoherent.lean` for the central
constructions used here.

## 2. The Majorana-1 chip in 200 words

Microsoft Quantum announced the **Majorana-1** chip in February 2024
([Microsoft Azure Quantum blog]; supporting peer-reviewed work in
Aghaee et al., *Nature* 638, 651–655 (2025)).  It is the first publicly-
demonstrated **topological qubit** based on Majorana zero modes (MZMs) at
the ends of semiconductor-superconductor (InAs/Al) nanowire heterostructures.

The basic logical unit is the **tetron** (Karzig et al., *Phys. Rev. B* 95,
235305 (2017); arXiv:1610.05289): four MZMs `γ₁, γ₂, γ₃, γ₄`, typically two
at each end of two parallel nanowires bridged by a superconducting island
that imposes a Coulomb-blockade fixed-parity constraint.  The four MZMs
generate a Clifford algebra; the **logical qubit** lives in the
2-dimensional fixed-total-parity subspace of the 4-dimensional Fock space.
Logical Pauli operators are *pair-parity bilinears* `iγ_aγ_b`.

The chip arranges several tetrons in a small graph, with edges = tunable
two-tetron couplings via "trijunctions".  Microsoft's announced 8-qubit
roadmap is a path-graph of 8 tetrons.  Gates are implemented by
**measurement-based braiding** (Aasen et al. arXiv:1511.05153): a
sequence of joint-parity measurements whose Kraus operators implement the
braid-group representation on the logical subspace.

## 3. Why this is a Tower 6 + 7 application

Three reasons.

**(a) Tower 3 captures the chip operator algebra.**  The chip Hamiltonian is
parity-conserving at its operating point.  The joint-parity-sector
projectors `Π_s = ∏_v (1 + (-1)^{s_v} P_v)/2`, indexed by `s : V → ZMod 2`,
are mutually orthogonal idempotents summing to 1 — a `CellProjectorSystem`
of `Graphplay/Dowsing/NonCommutativeCoherent.lean` §1.  The operator system
generated by `1`, the chip Hamiltonian, and the per-tetron parity operators
is parity-conserving, hence forms a `QuantumEquitablePartition` whose cells
are exactly the joint-parity sectors.  Inside each fixed-total-parity sector
the **quotient** is a direct sum of 2×2 effective Pauli Hamiltonians per
tetron, dressed by trijunction couplings.

**(b) Tower 6 captures the parameter-drift sheaf.**  The chip operates by
landing in an open region of the parameter manifold (chemical potential,
Zeeman field, magnetic flux through each superconducting loop) inside which
each nanowire is in the topological phase.  This parameter manifold `X`
supports a Tower-6 **`SheafGraph X`**: each open `U ⊆ X` carries the
chip operator algebra at the generic parameter in `U`, and the *nominal*
Hamiltonian is the global section.  Flux drift is a **non-trivial section**
of this sheaf.  Topological protection — the fact that small drift does not
destroy the parity-sector partition — becomes **flatness** of the U(1)
connection on this sheaf, in the precise sense of
`Graphplay/Integrations/LatticeGauge.lean`.

**(c) Tower 7 captures the braid-group representation.**  A braid sequence
of `n` strands is, in `Graphplay/Integrations/TQFT.lean`, a
`BraidRepresentation`: a homomorphism `B_n → U(ℋ)` whose action factors
through the cell-uniform subspace of the anyonic decoration (Theorem
`braid_factors_through_cellUniform`).  In Tower-7 language this is a
1-equivalence in the hom-bicategory of the parity-sector quotient; the
*topological* protection of the gate is the homotopy-class-invariance of the
braid in the configuration space of MZMs.

## 4. Three engineering payoffs as theorems

The Lean file `Graphplay/Applications/MajoranaOne.lean` states the
following three theorems (proofs `sorry`).

**Payoff 1 — Topologically-protected PST between two tetrons via braiding.**
For a parity-conserving chip with parity-sector equitable partition `P` and
a braid gate `BG : TQFT.BraidGate P` realising a permutation of sectors
`s_u ↔ s_v`, the chip exhibits cell-uniform PST between the cells at `s_u`
and `s_v` at time `BG.τ`.  Mechanism: combine `TQFT.braid_gate_realizable`
(existence of a realising Hamiltonian time) with `EquitablePartition.pst_lift`
(Bachman-Tamon 1108.0339 cell-uniform lift).

**Payoff 2 — Parity-symmetric noise preserves the cell-uniform subspace.**
If `N : NoiseModel V` is *parity-conserving* — every Lindblad jump operator
commutes with every per-tetron parity operator — then `N` is
`cellUniformSymmetric` with respect to the parity-sector partition `P`, and
hence the Lindblad evolution preserves `cellUniform P`
(`Graphplay/Toolkit/Noise.lean`).  Equivalently in D8 language
(`Graphplay/Dowsing/NoiseEquitable.lean`): `N.BreakingScore P = 0`.
Physically: any noise process conserving total fermion parity is
automatically parity-symmetric, and topological-gap protection exponentially
suppresses the parity-breaking processes.

**Payoff 3 — Drift in flux preserves equitability iff connection is flat.**
For a perturbed flux configuration `F : C.fluxField`, the parity-sector
equitable partition is preserved by the flux-dressed Hamiltonian iff `F` is
flat in the U(1) lattice-gauge sense.  This is the rigorous form of
"topological protection requires flat connections", obtained by combining
`Tower6.robust_pst_neighbourhood` with `LatticeGauge.signedBy_preserves_
equitable`.

## 5. Honest assessment: defensible vs. speculative

**Directly following from existing Graphplay theorems:**

* The parity-sector decomposition *is* a `QuantumEquitablePartition` once
  one accepts parity-conservation of the operator system.  This is the
  D5 non-commutative-coherent-algebra correspondence applied verbatim.
* Payoff 2 is *almost* a tautology: any symmetry-respecting noise model
  preserves the symmetry's cell-uniform subspace.  This is the direct
  application of `cellUniformSymmetric` + the parity-superselection structure.
* The braid-gate correspondence is `TQFT.braid_factors_through_cellUniform`
  applied to the parity-sector decoration.

**Speculative — modelling assumptions:**

* That the *physical* Majorana-1 Hamiltonian is faithfully captured by a
  parity-conserving Tower-3 operator system in the first place.  The
  literature supports this at the level of *effective* low-energy theory
  inside the topological phase; quasiparticle-poisoning corrections lie
  outside our Tower-3 picture.
* That measurement-based braiding (the chip's actual gate primitive) is
  *literally* the same data as a `TQFT.BraidRepresentation`.  Karzig et al.
  2017 prove that the measurement Kraus algebra realises a projective
  braid representation, which is what we use, but the equivalence at the
  *operator-system* level is implicit.
* That the magnetic-flux drift manifold is the *right* parameter manifold
  for the Tower-6 sheaf — as opposed to, say, the larger calibration
  manifold including chemical potential and Zeeman field.  This is a
  modelling choice.

**Speculative — sharpness claim:**

* That "topological protection = flatness of the Tower-6 connection" is the
  *sharpest* mathematical formulation of physical topological protection.
  Standard physics statements involve the adiabatic theorem and Berry
  phase; ours involves U(1) gauge-flatness of a sheaf-of-operator-algebras
  connection.  These should be equivalent in nice cases but the literature
  uses very different vocabulary.

## 6. Open questions

1. **Quasiparticle-poisoning as Tower-6 obstruction.**  Quasiparticle
   poisoning is *exactly* the failure of parity conservation.  In
   Tower-6 language, it is a non-vanishing class in `H¹(X; PST_failure)` of
   `Tower6.open_direction_cohomology`.  Can one compute this class
   explicitly for the Majorana-1 layout?
2. **Heawood-genus envelope.**  Is the Majorana-1 trijunction graph
   embeddable in a low-genus surface in such a way that its Heawood
   chromatic envelope matches a known MTC?  This is
   `TQFT.openQ1_HeawoodMatchesAnyons` specialised to the chip's planar
   topology.  For a path-8 layout the underlying topology is genus 0, so
   the question is trivial; for a more elaborate 2D tetron lattice it
   becomes non-trivial.
3. **Functoriality across chip generations.**  Microsoft's 8-qubit chip is
   one of a planned sequence of designs.  Can Tower-5's
   `Quotient.preservesFilteredColimits` be used to *transfer* a gate-design
   theorem from an `n`-tetron chip to an `(n+1)`-tetron chip, via the
   filtered colimit along chip-extension arrows?
4. **Bicategorical truncation for measurement-based braiding.**
   Measurement-based braiding produces a *projective* unitary (up to a
   measurement-outcome-dependent Pauli correction).  In Tower 7 this
   correction is a 2-cell in `BicategoricalEquitablePartition`.  Can
   `Tower7.bicategorical_lift` give a clean statement of the correction
   bookkeeping?
5. **Falsifiable: is the chip operator system actually parity-conserving?**
   Predicted by the present framework: the logical error rate of the chip
   should track `BreakingScore P` of the chip's noise model
   (`NoiseEquitable.lean`).  This is *directly testable* once chip
   randomized-benchmarking data is public.
